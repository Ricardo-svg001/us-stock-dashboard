## The conclusion first

The US market spends most of its time rising or consolidating near highs. Genuine deep corrections, bear markets and basing periods take up comparatively little time. So a market read shouldn't treat every dip to the 50-day average as a bear signal.

This site gives three moving averages three separate jobs:

- **50MA — tailwinds and normal pullbacks.** The index above its 50MA, with the 50MA above the 100MA, means the trend is intact.
- **100MA — genuine headwinds.** Only when the index breaks below the 100MA does the risk of a meaningful mid-term drawdown rise sharply.
- **150MA — market breadth and washouts.** The share of the top 300 constituents trading above their own 150MA, used mainly to infer whether the market has been thoroughly flushed.

Moving averages are not crystal balls. This framework doesn't guess tomorrow's direction. It answers one question in plain terms: is this a time to trade normally with the trend, to stop chasing, or to prioritise defence?

## 1. Why breaking the 50MA isn't a headwind

The 50MA covers roughly ten weeks of trading. It reacts quickly, which makes it good for telling whether the dominant trend is still running smoothly.

But inside the long-term uptrend structure of US equities, the first retest of the 50MA after a strong advance is usually just normal consolidation. It signals that short-term momentum has cooled and volatility may pick up. It does not mean the mid-term trend has reversed.

So this site calls "back to the 50MA but holding the 100MA" a **bull pullback**, not a headwind market. It's a moment to stop chasing, cut position size, or wait for individual names to steady — not a reason to turn bearish across the board.

## 2. The 100MA is the mid-term risk line

The 100MA covers about five months of trading. It's slower than the 50MA, but far better at separating a normal bull pullback from a deeper correction.

When the Nasdaq Composite breaks its 100MA, short- and mid-term participants move toward or into losses, rallies run into supply more easily, and the odds of a larger drawdown rise noticeably. That's why this site labels anything below the 100MA a **headwind market**.

Headwind doesn't mean the decline must continue, and it certainly doesn't mean you should short. Post-bear rallies in US equities can be fast and large. The label exists to remind you to reduce exposure, avoid averaging down, and wait for the trend to repair.

## 3. Tangled averages are a high-risk consolidation

When the index, the 50MA and the 100MA cluster together, or the alignment sends conflicting signals, the market is switching direction across multiple timeframes. It can resolve upward or downward — "everyone's cost basis is similar" is not evidence of an imminent crash.

This site calls that environment a **high-risk consolidation**. It doesn't imply a crash, but with short- and mid-term cost bases in conflict, the sensible response is to reduce leverage and concentration, then wait either for a proper 50MA-above-100MA alignment to reform, or for a break of the 100MA to confirm headwinds.

## 4. Market breadth: the share above their own 150MA

The Nasdaq Composite is capitalisation-weighted. A handful of large constituents rising can make the index look strong while many stocks underneath have already rolled over.

So this site separately computes:

**Among the 300 largest constituents, what share are trading above their own 150-day moving average?**

- Breadth 80%: about 240 names above their 150MA — participation is broad.
- Breadth 50%: roughly half strong, half weak.
- Breadth 30%: only about 90 names above their 150MA — most stocks have already broken their mid-to-long-term trend.

High breadth can persist for a long time in a bull market, so it can't be used on its own to call a top. This site pays more attention to low breadth: when the lowest breadth reading over the past 90 trading days has dipped **below 30%**, most constituents have been flushed.

Low breadth is evidence of a washout, not a buy signal. Markets can fall a great deal and keep falling — so it has to be paired with the index reclaiming its averages to judge how far a recovery has actually progressed.

## 5. The market lifecycle: six positions

The lifecycle turns an abstract bull/bear call into a map: **early recovery → confirmed recovery → tailwind trend → bull pullback → high-risk consolidation → headwind market**.

That order describes a common shape. **It is not a prediction that markets must follow the sequence.** Stages can be skipped or revisited — a bull pullback that holds the 50MA can go straight back to a tailwind trend without passing through a high-risk consolidation. The home page marks where you are now; it does not announce the next stop.

### 🟢 Tailwind trend

The index is above the 50MA and the 50MA is above the 100MA. The short- and mid-term trend is intact, leading stocks tend to keep working, and the watchlist and pullback screeners can be used normally.

### 🟡 Bull pullback

The 50MA is still above the 100MA, but the index has slipped below the 50MA while holding the 100MA. This is normal consolidation within an uptrend. Stop chasing, wait for individual names to steady, and don't read it as a bear market.

### 🟠 High-risk consolidation

The index is still above the 100MA, but the 50MA/100MA alignment is conflicted. The market is choosing a direction — reduce leverage and wait for confirmation.

### 🔴 Headwind market

The index has broken the 100MA. Mid-term drawdown risk is elevated. Control size, respect stops, and don't average down just because prices have already fallen a lot.

### 🔵 Early recovery

Breadth dipped below 30% at some point in the last 90 trading days, and the index has reclaimed the 50MA but not yet the 100MA. The market is bouncing after a flush. Build a watchlist, but the mid-term trend is not confirmed.

### 🔵 Confirmed recovery

Breadth dipped below 30% in the last 90 trading days, and the index has reclaimed the 100MA. The mid-term trend is improving after the washout — though bear rallies and retests remain possible, and there's no guarantee the low is in.

## 6. Why the state doesn't flip around daily

A market state has to hold for three consecutive trading days before it switches. A single day above or below an average won't change the home page's conclusion.

Three-day confirmation reduces false breaks; it does not eliminate false signals. In a fast crash, every moving average is late. Real tail risk still has to be handled with position sizing, diversification and stops.

## 7. How to use it day to day

1. Check the market weather on the home page: tailwind, pullback, consolidation, headwind or recovery.
2. Check 150MA breadth and its 90-day low to see whether the market has been properly flushed.
3. Only then move to the watchlist or pullback screener for individual names.

The single most important rule: **a dip to the 50MA is a normal bull pullback; a break of the 100MA is the headwind zone that requires defence. 150MA breadth is for judging washouts and recoveries, nothing else.**

Historical breadth is computed from *today's* 300 largest constituents, which introduces survivorship bias — earlier readings may be overstated. This article and the tools on this site describe the market environment and are not investment advice.
