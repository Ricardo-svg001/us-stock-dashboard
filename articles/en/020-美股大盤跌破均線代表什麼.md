## Direction matters as much as the break

Brief moves below an average are common. More useful questions are whether the averages still rise, whether their alignment remains constructive, and whether price can quickly reclaim the level.

When an index is consolidating around rising intermediate-term averages, a decline can be ordinary profit-taking rather than a trend failure. That is a reason to avoid chasing, not necessarily to treat every holding as a bear-market trade.

## Three signals to monitor

- **Average turns:** A short-term average rolling over and crossing below an intermediate average can mean repair will take longer.
- **Rebound quality:** Heavy-volume selling followed by weak rallies below the old support suggests that support may have become resistance.
- **Market breadth:** If more large stocks fall below their own long-term averages while the headline index appears stable, weakness may be spreading below the surface.

The home-page market lifecycle and breadth measures put these signals in one place. They describe the current environment; they do not call an exact top or bottom.

## What to do with individual stocks

During market weakness, compare stocks with the index. Names that hold key averages, rebound first and retain healthy revenue and earnings trends may be worth keeping on a watchlist. Relative strength is still not a guarantee: even the strongest names can fall in a broad risk-off market. Position size and patience remain part of the decision.
