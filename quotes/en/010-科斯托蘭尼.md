## The Speculator
Those with money can speculate; those with little money must not; those with no money have to.
A successful speculator wins 51 out of 100 times and loses 49 — he lives off that difference.
For a true speculator, what matters is not just the thrill of winning, but being proved right.
For a speculator, 20% of life is the market; for a gambler, it is 80%.

## Certainty & Randomness
Reality is usually the last thing anyone expects. That is why officials, engineers, economists, executives and company heads are the least capable of diagnosing the market.
If you want a stable investment: buy the stocks, take a sleeping pill, sleep for ten years, and wake up to a highly certain result.
2 + 2 = 5 − 1. The result is what you expected, but the path never is.

## News & Information
Whatever everyone on the exchange already knows does not excite me.
Even as a child I learned you cannot trade on the day's headlines.
To make money in the market, you do not necessarily need the most accurate information.
On the exchange, “well informed” is a synonym for “ruined”.
The most accurate inside information can, in some circumstances, be the most damaging.
Almost anything can happen in the market — even the logical.

## Supply, Demand & Judgement
Buying stocks needs romantic imagination; selling needs cold realism. In between, you should be asleep.
The market rises or falls on supply and demand: are there more fools, or more shares?
The world needs moderate inflation — it is like a pleasant warm bath. But keep heating it and the tub eventually explodes.
A person of true conviction is one who can decline an invitation without needing a reason.
Do less arithmetic and more thinking — the numbers are only the surface.
The market is not a science but an art. Like painting, trading needs a touch of surrealism.

## Patience & Waiting
For a speculator, thinking without acting is better than acting without thinking.
Never chase a tram or a stock. Be patient; the next one always comes.

## Reading a Rise
Light volume with the index still rising is very favourable.
Look at the trend before the stock. In an uptrend even a poor speculator makes money; in a downtrend even a good stock will not.

## The Price of Optimism
When every speculator piles into what everyone agrees is a sure thing, the outcome is almost always failure.
Only losses are real. Profits are an illusion — money merely borrowed, which the next upheaval usually demands back in full.
When the market stops responding to good news, that is the signal of a top.
The day the optimists turn pessimistic may be the turning point. But when the born pessimists turn optimistic, get out as fast as you can.
Bull markets are born in panic, grow on fear, mature in optimism and end in euphoria.

## Declines & Losses
Winning is possible, losing is inevitable, winning it back is impossible.
Light volume with the index still falling means the outlook is poor.
Only someone who has far more money than he borrows may borrow to buy stocks.
Better to own a few shares of a debt-laden company with your own money than shares of a first-class company on credit.
A stock may eventually rise ten-thousand percent, but it can only fall one hundred.
If you bet too much on one speculation, or cannot withstand even a small adverse move, then even the sharpest judgement will eventually lose everything.
The two hardest things in the market: accepting a loss, and not taking a small profit. Harder still is having an independent view.

## Courage at the Bottom
The great misfortune of the old speculator: he has accumulated experience but lost his courage.
When bad news fails to push the market down, it is a sign the market is oversold and near a bottom.
Heavy volume during a decline means large amounts of stock are moving from weak hands to strong ones.
Ride out the cycle — experience tells me 90% of the market are cowards, and at most 10% are the steadfast.
Rises and falls are inseparable. If you cannot see where the fall ends, you cannot see where the rise begins.
A crash is usually the prelude to a boom; a boom usually ends in a crash.
